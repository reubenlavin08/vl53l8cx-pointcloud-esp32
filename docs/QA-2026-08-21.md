# P0 session — your questions answered + rulings logged — 2026-08-21

## 1. CLOSEST vs STRONGEST — your skepticism is legitimate; here's the split

Your instinct is right that CLOSEST can be less stable: a weak near
return flickering in/out makes the zone's range jump, and that would
pollute the CV distance readouts. STRONGEST is stable but **silently
invisible to thin near obstacles** (a 3 cm branch fills ~2% of a zone —
the wall behind always wins).

**The resolution is we don't have to choose.** The sensor supports
multi-target per zone: set order = CLOSEST **and** read 2 targets.
Then:
- **CV/fusion distances keep using the stable surface** (pick the
  target with the higher signal rate — behaves like STRONGEST).
- **Safety checks watch target 0 (the closest)** — a persistent nearer
  return in the head band triggers the hazard path.
- Our existing 0.8 s valid-hold median already smooths flicker, and
  `target_status` filtering rejects most junk returns.

Plan: A/B capture (20 s each config at the desk + a broom-handle "thin
branch") before committing. It's one config bit + one API call.

## 2. IMU mount calibration — your 2 minutes, exactly

The helmet must stream over **USB serial** for this (script reads COM
directly — close cv_fusion first so the port is free):

1. Plug the helmet into USB (check the COM number in Device Manager).
2. `python visualizer/imu_mount_cal.py --port COM9` (or whatever COM).
3. **Step 1**: hold the helmet LEVEL — as it sits on a head looking at
   the horizon. Press Enter, keep it still 4 s.
4. **Step 2**: pitch it NOSE-DOWN ~90° (wearer looking at their feet).
   Enter, still 4 s.
5. It prints the mount matrix, snaps to the nearest cardinal mount, and
   saves `visualizer/imu_mount_cal.json`.
6. **Live verification**: move the helmet and watch — nose-down = pitch
   positive, right-ear-down = roll positive, left turn = yaw positive.
   If any axis reads wrong, redo (you probably weren't level in step 1).

That's it. This unlocks: correct HUD labels, gravity-frame
head-clearance, pose-aware floor rejection, the leveling coach's
accuracy, and the beacon's lost-target tracking.

## 3. Why the sunlight bench test matters (15 min, free)

The datasheet says max range on a grey target collapses from **2.4 m in
darkness to ~0.9–1.0 m at 5,000 lux** — and outdoor daylight is
50,000–100,000 lux. Every outdoor safety feature we planned
(head-clearance first-alert at 2 m, drop-off warning at 2.5 m) assumes
range we may simply not have in sunlight. If outdoors the ToF sees
0.9 m, warnings fire as you arrive at the hazard — too late — and the
outdoor feature set needs redesign (camera-led, ToF as near confirm).

The test: tape measure + a cardboard target, note max reliable range
indoors, in shade, and in direct sun. One number changes every outdoor
design decision. That's why it's P0.

## 5. Bone conduction — good news, Bluetooth is FINE for v1

Walking back my own caution with the actual numbers, feature by
feature:

| Channel | Needs low latency? | On Bluetooth |
|---|---|---|
| Speech callouts | No | ✅ perfect |
| Proximity ticker | No — it's MONO (rate carries the info, not direction) | ✅ perfect |
| Soundscape beacon | Partly | ✅ usable — see below |
| Future precise HRTF clicks | Yes (<30 ms) | ❌ degraded |

The beacon tolerates Bluetooth better than I first said: its regions
are 40–70° wide and switches are already quantized to ~0.4 s beat
boundaries by design, so a ~200 ms lag mostly hides inside behavior it
already has. Only the stereo pan lags noticeably during fast head
turns.

**So: buy a cheap Bluetooth bone-conduction set and skip wired
entirely.** From your own search results, best candidate: **TECKNET
BT bone conduction, $33.99, 1,261 reviews, two-day** (the $19.99
20-review one is a gamble; fine as a backup). Everything we built
works with it today — and the laptop speakers work for testing
meanwhile.
